The Fairway Logbook

Cross-Device UX Review & Redesign

A frictionless on-course system across iPhone, Apple Watch, and Even Realities G2 glasses — plus the course-data desk that feeds them. This issue diagnoses where the round breaks down today and proposes the fixes, screen by screen.

The one thing to fix

Stop asking the golfer to stand on the spot and tap.

Your sharpest friction — “I forget to mark the stroke from where I hit, so the club-distance feature is a wash” — is the exact gap between 18Birdies (manual) and Arccos (automatic). Arccos closes it with a sensor in every grip. You don't have that hardware, but you have three things that, combined, get you most of the way there:

1 A continuous GPS breadcrumb the phone already records — the track itself knows where you walked and stopped.
2 The watch's swing detection — a timestamp for each impact, even when the phone's in your pocket.
3 Known hole geometry — tee and green anchors from your course-data desk bound the start and end of every hole.

Cross-reference the three and marking becomes confirming. You play the hole, and when you hole out the app says “we tracked 4 shots” on a map you fix in five seconds. Forgetting can no longer cost you the data.

Verdict at a glance
iPhone
Beautiful, but the on-course screen optimizes for marking, not glancing. Make yardage the hero; make marking retroactive.
Apple Watch
Your best input device — lean into it. Make the confirm card auto-accept so a missed tap costs nothing.
G2 Glasses
Output-only is correct. One job: the yardage number, bigger. Mirror the phone's hierarchy exactly.
Course Desk
Functional. Tighten the green-marking step; the anchors it produces are what make reconstruction work.

Section II · The Hero Fix

Marking becomes confirming

Today the round depends on a tap at every shot. The fix: the phone records your walk passively, and you settle the hole once at the end — never on the spot. There are two ways in, and they converge on the same card. With a watch, shots are pre-detected and you just confirm. Phone-only, your score is the input — you enter the number you'd write down anyway, and the app lays that many pins along your track.

Today · capture model
Walk to ball → remember to open phone → stand on exact spot → tap Log Shot → pick club. Miss one step and the hole's distances are gone. The data is only as good as your memory mid-swing.
Proposed · confirm model
Just play; the phone records the walk in your pocket. At hole-out you confirm once — with a watch the shots are already detected; phone-only, you enter your score and the app drops that many pins on the track. Either way, nothing to remember mid-swing.

No watch? The phone-only path

Your score is the only thing you enter

Without a watch there's no swing to detect — so the app doesn't pretend to. You finish the hole and enter your score, exactly like 18Birdies. That single number is the whole interaction; the reconstructed shots stay collapsed behind one tap, so a score-only round never sees a pin. Expand it only when you want club data — the app has already laid the shots along the track it recorded, ready to nudge.

Section V · Apple Watch

Your best input device — make it cost nothing

The watch is where reconstruction earns its accuracy: it timestamps every swing from your wrist while the phone sits in your pocket. The current 5-second confirm card is good — but it asks for an action. Flip it: auto-accept the detection on a timeout, so a swing you ignore is still recorded. The card becomes a chance to correct, not a checkpoint to clear.

The armed-club chip = the answer to “which club?”
The face always shows a club, predicted from your distance-to-green using the averages the app is learning. It's never blank, so a detected swing always has a club to attach. Crown to change it; it's a default, never a chore.
Putts: green-aware, one tap each
Swings can't be felt on a putt, so the watch doesn't try. When GPS places you on the green it auto-swaps to a putt counter — tap per putt, then Holed out. Fills the gap between your last full shot and the cup.
One number, not three
The watch shows only the to-green (middle) distance — front/back are dropped here to keep the face glanceable. The same single number appears on the phone and glasses.

Section VI · Even Realities G2

The whole hole, without reaching for a wrist

You want the glasses to carry everything that would otherwise make you glance down — so they become your primary read, watch and phone the backups. Output-only still holds: no menus, no input at address. The trick is a strict hierarchy on the 576×288 monochrome canvas. The to-green number is the hero; a thin top and bottom frame carry the rest — par, stroke, armed club, and your last shot — each in its own fixed corner so your eye always knows where to look.

Section VII · Course Desk (web)

The anchors that make reconstruction possible

This tool is off-course prep, so a visual restyle is optional. But its output is load-bearing: the tee and green anchors it publishes are what let the phone bound each hole and place pins. Two UX upgrades pay for themselves on every round.

Upgrade 1
Mark the tee box, not just the green
You already drop a middle-of-green marker — keep it as the only green point. Add a tee anchor per hole: that's the second bookend reconstruction needs to bound each hole's track. Green + tee is enough; no front/back required.
Upgrade 2
Completeness checklist before publish
A simple 18-row status grid — par ✓, tee ✓, green ✓ — so a half-marked course can't silently ship and leave you with no yardage on the 14th.
Optional If you do restyle it, the lightest touch — Georgia headings, the cream/ink palette, Courier Prime labels — makes the desk feel like the same product without rebuilding the map editor.
